MongoDB Atlas · Partner Guidance

M0 is for learning. Your customers are building real products.

If your product provisions MongoDB Atlas for customers, every workload that serves real users or real business data belongs on a paid tier (M10+ dedicated, or Flex for lightweight dev/test). This guide shows what M0 blocks, why paid tiers belong in dev and stage too, and exactly how to get each customer there.

512 MB storage cap on M0 500 max connections on M0 0 backups · uptime SLA on M0 M10+ unlocks the full platform

Technical guidance only — MongoDB reviews and approves your confirmation screen and disclosure language before launch. Building these flows does not by itself qualify the partner for any incentives, which separate written terms would cover.

M0 vs paid clusters — the real difference

M0 is a sandbox: shared resources, hard caps, and no safety net. Paid tiers are the same database engine with the controls real applications need.

Capability 🆓M0 — Free 〰️Flex ⚡M10+ — Dedicated
Storage 512 MB 5 GB 10 GB and up, with storage auto-scaling
Compute Shared RAM & vCPU Shared, burstable Dedicated RAM & vCPUs — predictable performance
Max connections 500 500 1,500+, scaling with tier
Auto-scaling ✗ None ✗ Manual only ✓ Compute and storage auto-scaling
Backups ✗ No backups ✓ Daily backups ✓ Customizable backups & restore options
Uptime SLA ✗ None ✗ None ✓ Production uptime SLA
Performance tooling Basic monitoring only Basic monitoring only ✓ Performance Advisor, profiler, real-time metrics
Private networking ✗ ✗ ✓ VPC peering / private endpoints
Auditing & customer-managed keys ✗ ✗ ✓ On eligible dedicated tiers
Search indexes 3 10 Unlimited — room for AI/vector workloads

Specs as shown in the Atlas tier picker (see the upgrade screenshot below). Dedicated tiers scale well beyond M10 — storage, RAM, vCPUs, and connection limits all grow with tier size.

Why paid tiers — even for dev and stage

An M0 dev environment hides problems until production. Paid tiers in every environment let your customers use the full potential of MongoDB from day one — and promote to production without a migration.

🚦

Representative load testing

Dedicated RAM/vCPUs and 1,500+ connections mean performance numbers that actually mean something. M0's shared compute and 500-connection ceiling throttle any serious test — so "fast enough on M0" proves nothing.

🛟

Backups in every environment

Practice restores in dev and stage before production depends on them. M0 has no backups at all — one bad migration or accidental delete and the data is gone.

📊

Full observability while building

Performance Advisor, the query profiler, and granular real-time metrics let your customers tune indexes and queries as they build — not as an emergency after launch.

🔐

Security parity with production

Private networking, auditing, and customer-managed keys can only be exercised on paid tiers. If stage can't mirror prod's security posture, the go-live review is where surprises surface.

🧠

AI-ready capacity

M0 allows just 3 search indexes — AI features built on Atlas Search and Vector Search quickly outgrow that. Paid tiers offer unlimited indexes and the memory headroom embedding and retrieval workloads need.

🪜

A clean promotion path

Dev → stage → prod on the same tier family means resizing in place, not migrating off M0 in the middle of a launch. The cluster your customer tests is the cluster they ship.

💡 Rule of thumb for your customers
M0 for a weekend prototype or a first look at MongoDB. The moment a partner demo, a customer, or an employee depends on the application — in any environment — it belongs on a paid tier.

Make the recommendation specific

To recommend the right tier, align on a few things together with the customer:

🧭 Align on these together

Is this still a prototype, or will customers and employees rely on it?
What traffic, connection levels, data growth, and peak periods do you expect?
Will the application store sensitive or business-critical information?
Do you require private networking, auditing, customer-managed keys, or dedicated workload isolation?
What recovery expectations do you have — and have you tested the application with representative traffic?

Getting your customer to a paid tier

Two situations, three routes each — pick whichever fits the experience: inside your product with the Partner API, through an AI client with Remote MCP, or directly in the Atlas UI.

💳
Billing, said plainly: paid clusters are billed to the customer's own Atlas organization from the moment a cluster is created or an upgrade is applied. Whatever the route, confirm the tier, region, and expected charges with the customer first — they approve the spend, and the spend is theirs.
A

The customer already has an M0 cluster

Upgrade it in place to M10 — data carries over, no migration needed. Expand a route to see its requirements and steps.

⚠️ Before any upgrade — the fine print your customer must hear
  • Downtime is required for M0 → Flex/dedicated. Halt writes throughout the operation and don't promise a fixed duration — plan for roughly ~10 minutes while the tier scales. Atlas migrates the live data but replaces the source deployment; restart application clients afterwards and verify before resuming writes.
  • M0 has no managed snapshots — export anything important beforehand with mongodump. If backups were enabled on the Free cluster, its existing snapshots are deleted after the upgrade and can't be recovered — download or restore what you need first.
  • Not reversible: a dedicated cluster can't be downgraded in place back to M0 or Flex. Don't present the upgrade as freely reversible.
  • The oplog is not preserved when moving to dedicated — affected Database Triggers must be resumed without their old resume tokens.
  • OAuth authorization ≠ spending consent. Roles, organization delegation settings, valid billing, and supported tier/provider/region combinations still apply. No silent agent upgrades, no reselling, no markup — usage is billed to the customer's Atlas organization, and the price must be disclosed before every paid operation.

🤝 Route 2 · Partner API — trigger the upgrade inside your product

›

Requirements

Approved partner onboarding + registered OAuth client User with Project Cluster Manager Org allows third-party app connections Payment method on file in Atlas
Customer chooses "Upgrade MongoDB" in your product. In your database/integration settings, let the customer select the connected Atlas organization, project, and existing M0 cluster. Show the current tier and make clear they are upgrading that cluster — not creating a new one. If Atlas isn't connected yet, run the Partner API OAuth 2.1 Authorization Code + PKCE flow: redirect to https://cloud.mongodb.com/oauth/authorize (response_type=code, client_id, registered redirect_uri, code_challenge, code_challenge_method=S256, unique state); validate state and the iss issuer on callback; exchange the code at POST https://authorize.mongodb.com/tokens (grant_type=authorization_code, code, code_verifier, redirect_uri, client_id, plus client_secret for confidential clients). Store tokens securely and use the access token as Authorization: Bearer ….
Discover the customer's M0 cluster with the delegated token: GET /api/atlas/v2/orgs → GET /api/atlas/v2/orgs/{orgId}/groups → GET /api/atlas/v2/groups/{groupId}/clusters. Confirm the selected cluster is Free/M0 and capture its name and project ID. If no organization is returned, guide the customer to ask an Organization Owner to enable third-party app connections (Organization Settings → App Connections). The authorizing user needs at least Project Cluster Manager on the project.
Explain the paid change and obtain confirmation. Before any billable call, show the MongoDB-approved disclosure (below): the selected cluster and destination tier (e.g. M10), the provider/region and configuration, a link to MongoDB pricing, a statement that charges are billed directly to the customer's Atlas organization, and the expected downtime and snapshot warnings. Customer confirmation is required — never let an AI agent silently initiate a paid upgrade.
Check billing readiness. A payment method is configured at the Atlas organization level. The Partner API does not redirect the customer's browser to Atlas Billing — if the org has no payment method, the upgrade request fails with 402 NO_PAYMENT_INFORMATION_FOUND (see the handoff below). Don't retry automatically; send the customer to Atlas Billing, then let them resume with selections preserved and confirm again.
Submit the upgrade — the documented tenant-upgrade endpoint, not create-cluster:
HTTP — Upgrade One Shared-Tier Cluster
POST https://cloud.mongodb.com/api/atlas/v2/groups/{groupId}/clusters/tenantUpgrade
Authorization: Bearer <ACCESS_TOKEN>
Accept: application/vnd.atlas.2023-01-01+json
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "name": "<existing-m0-cluster-name>",
  "providerSettings": {
    "providerName": "AWS",
    "instanceSizeName": "M10"
  }
}
Handle the asynchronous upgrade. Poll the cluster status until stateName is IDLE and show progress. Handle authorization, permission, validation, and billing failures in your UI. When it finishes, prompt the customer to restart application clients, check the connection, and verify the app before resuming writes. Don't invent or reuse a cluster-create payload — this is the tenant-upgrade operation.
💳 The missing-billing handoff (402) — you own it, not Atlas

If the customer's Atlas organization has no payment method on file, the upgrade (or create) request returns:

Response — 402 Payment Required
{
  "error": 402,
  "reason": "Payment Required",
  "detail": "No payment information was found for group <groupId>. Go to
    https://cloud.mongodb.com/v2#/org/<orgId>/checkout?type=editPayment
    to add a payment method in Atlas.",
  "errorCode": "NO_PAYMENT_INFORMATION_FOUND"
}
  • Stop the flow — do not retry the upgrade automatically; it cannot succeed until a payment method is added.
  • Tell the customer clearly that a payment method is required and that it is added in Atlas, not in your product.
  • Deep-link to Atlas Billing for their organization (https://cloud.mongodb.com/v2#/org/<orgId>/checkout?type=editPayment). Adding it requires the Organization Owner or Organization Billing Admin role (Organization → Billing → Payment Method → Edit → Save Payment Method) — if the user can't, point them to their Organization Owner.
  • Provide a way back. When the customer returns, restore the same upgrade screen with selections preserved and require explicit confirmation again before resubmitting.
  • Never collect or store card details — payment information is entered only in Atlas.

The published documentation describes an in-product payment prompt in the Atlas UI; it does not document an automatic Billing-page redirect for the Partner API. Implement the manual handoff and integration-test the exact billing-readiness behavior before launch.

🤖 Route 3 · Remote MCP — from an AI client or operator workflow

›

Requirements

MCP deployment with atlas-upgrade-cluster available AI client access enabled for the org, or an MCP configuration for the project Customer approval
Connect a supported AI client to MongoDB Atlas Managed MCP — user-delegated (authorize in the browser) or an admin-provisioned MCP configuration authorized for the target project.
After confirming the project and the customer's explicit approval, call atlas-upgrade-cluster with target tier M10 — the tool supports Free-to-M10 upgrades. An agent must never initiate a paid upgrade without a human confirmation step.
Poll with atlas-inspect-cluster until the status is IDLE, then restart app clients and verify.
💳 Billing
Paid billing starts when the upgrade is applied — confirm tier and charges with the customer first, and never let an agent run a paid upgrade without an explicit human approval step (OAuth authorization is not spending consent). Confirm atlas-upgrade-cluster exists in your MCP deployment before relying on it (see the MCP Server tool reference).

🤝 Partner API — embedded "Create cluster" flow

The customer authorizes your product to provision Atlas on their behalf.

Requirements

Approved partner onboarding + registered OAuth client Role that permits cluster creation Org allows third-party app connections
Authorize: redirect the customer to https://cloud.mongodb.com/oauth/authorize using OAuth 2.1 Authorization Code + PKCE (response_type=code, client_id, registered redirect_uri, code_challenge, code_challenge_method=S256, state).
Get a token: validate state and the iss issuer on the callback, then exchange the code at POST https://authorize.mongodb.com/tokens (grant_type=authorization_code, code, code_verifier, redirect_uri, client_id; plus client_secret for confidential clients). Use the returned access token as Authorization: Bearer ….
Select the project and configure: call GET /api/atlas/v2/orgs, then GET /api/atlas/v2/orgs/{orgId}/groups. Let the customer pick the project and configure the new cluster's name, tier, provider, region, and other supported settings. The customer needs a role that permits cluster creation, and the organization must allow third-party app connections.
Show the price and obtain confirmation: before the billable call, show the selected tier, provider, region, and configuration; link to the MongoDB pricing page; and state clearly that charges are billed directly to the customer's Atlas organization. Use the MongoDB-provided disclosure language (below) and get explicit confirmation immediately before submitting. MongoDB reviews and approves your confirmation screen before launch. Never let an AI agent silently create a paid cluster.
Check billing readiness: the Partner API does not redirect the customer to Atlas Billing. If the create request returns 402 NO_PAYMENT_INFORMATION_FOUND, stop, don't retry, and hand the customer off to Atlas Billing (https://cloud.mongodb.com/v2#/org/{orgId}/checkout?type=editPayment — Organization Owner or Organization Billing Admin role required). When they return, restore the configuration, show the price and disclosure again, and require fresh confirmation. Never collect or store card details in your product.
Create the cluster: after billing readiness and explicit confirmation, call POST /api/atlas/v2/groups/{groupId}/clusters with a body conforming to the Create One Cluster schema (name, type, replication spec for the selected paid tier). Do not use the tenantUpgrade endpoint — that is only for upgrading an existing shared-tier cluster. Poll GET …/clusters/{clusterName} until stateName is IDLE.
Connect securely: create a least-privilege DB user via POST …/databaseUsers, restrict network access via POST …/accessList, then read the connection string from the cluster resource and pass it to the app over a secure channel. Guide the customer through connecting and validating their application.
💳 Billing
Charges accrue to the customer's Atlas organization from the moment the cluster is created — always confirm tier, region, and expected charges in your UI before calling the create API.

🤖 Remote MCP — natural-language provisioning

An authorized AI client or operator provisions through MCP tools.

Requirements

MCP deployment with atlas-create-cluster available MCP configuration (client ID/secret + roles) for the project Customer approval of tier, region & charges
Connect a supported AI client to MongoDB Atlas Managed MCP — user-delegated (authorize in the browser) or an admin-provisioned MCP configuration with roles scoped to the target project.
Use atlas-list-orgs and atlas-list-projects to select the target project, and atlas-get-regions to resolve the region.
After the customer confirms tier, region, and billing, call atlas-create-cluster with the project, cluster name, M10 tier (or supported M10–M80), provider, region, and topology.
Poll with atlas-inspect-cluster until the status is IDLE.
Configure a least-privilege DB user and restricted network access, then retrieve and securely use the connection string.
💳 Billing
Charges accrue to the customer's Atlas organization from creation — confirm tier, region, and charges before running the tool. Confirm the desired tool exists in your MCP deployment before relying on it (see the MCP Server tool reference).
🖥️ Third option: Atlas UI
If your team is not yet onboarded as an approved Partner API design partner — or the customer simply wants direct control — the customer creates the paid cluster in the Atlas UI using the standard workflow, choosing M10 (or Flex for dev/test) at creation time. The Atlas picker shows the hourly price before they confirm, and billing goes to their own Atlas organization.

The confirmation screen: required disclosure

Before making available any functionality that creates, upgrades, or otherwise initiates billable resources in a customer's MongoDB account, submit your implementation of the MongoDB-provided disclosure language — screenshots or a test environment showing wording, placement, and user flow — to MongoDB for approval. The reference confirmation:

Reference confirmation dialog — adapt, then submit for approval

Clicking Confirm will [create a new cluster / upgrade a cluster] in your MongoDB Atlas project. This action is governed by your existing agreement with MongoDB. For pricing information visit MongoDB Pricing.

Cluster Tier:  [tier] 
Cloud Provider & Region:  [provider & region] 

MongoDB Atlas usage will be billed by MongoDB to the payment method for this organization, beginning immediately and continuing until you downgrade or delete the cluster.

Upgrading a Free cluster or a Flex cluster to an M10 or larger cluster tier requires downtime. You can't scale a dedicated cluster back to a Free cluster or a Flex cluster. Any Free cluster backups are not retained after upgrade.

🪪
Every paid operation, every time. Show this disclosure immediately before the billable API call — on the first attempt and on every retry after a billing handoff. Nothing happens until the customer confirms, and no AI agent may confirm on their behalf.

Partner implementation checklist

The non-negotiables before your paid-tier flow goes live.

  • Use the customer-delegated OAuth token; never collect the customer's Atlas password or payment-card details in the partner app.
  • Validate organization app-connections settings and Project Cluster Manager permissions before submitting the paid request.
  • Present tier/cost and obtain affirmative approval using MongoDB-provided disclosure language immediately before the call — and submit the screen to MongoDB for approval before launch.
  • Handle missing billing (402) as a customer handoff to Atlas Billing; do not assume the API redirects the browser, and never auto-retry a paid operation.
  • Securely store OAuth tokens and redact bearer tokens, credentials, and connection strings from logs.
  • After success, report completion and guide the customer through reconnecting and validating their app via your partner integration surface.

Which route should you recommend?

Match the route to the experience your customer is in.

Situation Recommended route
Customer wants a "Create MongoDB cluster" flow inside a partner product Partner API with delegated user authorization
Customer/operator wants natural-language Atlas administration from an MCP-connected AI client Remote MCP, if the required Atlas tools and credentials are configured
Partner does not have approved Partner API onboarding, or the customer wants direct control in Atlas Atlas UI using the standard create/upgrade workflow
🤝
In all cases: the customer owns the Atlas account and project, understands the paid-resource charges, approves the configuration before creation, and retains control over credentials and network access. M0 is where ideas start — paid tiers are where products run.
